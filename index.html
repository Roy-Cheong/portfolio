Work experience

Ericsson

Software Engineering Intern

Ericsson · Athlone, Ireland · January to September 2026

Ericsson builds the equipment and software behind mobile phone networks. Over nine months I worked on three early-stage prototypes, each testing whether an idea was worth developing further.

  1. 01

    A "digital twin" of a mobile network

    A digital twin is a software model of a real system, used to study how it behaves. I brought together features built by different team members into one shared codebase, and built a chatbot so people could ask questions about the results in plain English. I also helped analyse which network settings had the biggest effect on performance. The prototype was presented to senior engineering and customer-facing leadership.

  2. 02

    An AI assistant that runs on a local machine

    I prototyped a question-answering system that looks up answers in a knowledge base and runs on small AI models, so it does not depend on a large external service. I compared several small models on how good their answers were and how closely they stuck to the source material.

  3. 03

    A router that picks the right AI model for each question

    Large AI models give better answers but are slower and more costly to run. I designed and built a router that judges how difficult each question is, then sends simple ones to a small, fast model and harder ones to a larger model.

    Step 1A question comes in
    →
    Step 2The router judges how difficult it is
    →
    SimpleSmall, fast model (Gemma 2 2B)
    ComplexLarger model (Qwen3.5-9B)

What I took from it: working towards a presentation to senior leadership taught me to collaborate under a deadline and stay focused on the team's priorities. I came away with practical experience in combining other people's work into one product, applying AI to real problems and working inside an engineering team.

Smart Content Moderation Assistant

Personal project · May to September 2025

A web application that reads text messages and emails and decides whether each one is spam. I built both halves myself: the machine-learning models that make the decision and the screen people use to try it.

99%accuracy on 5,000 text messages
94%precision on 500,000 emails

The app shows how confident the model is in each decision and highlights the words that influenced it, so a person can check its reasoning. Users can also upload a whole file of messages and have them all checked at once. I published the trained models online for others to use.
